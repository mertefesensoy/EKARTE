## 6. Licensing takeaways

- Derivative-friendly content is scarce: WikEM (CC BY-SA, with an AI clause to negotiate) and Turk J Emerg Med (CC BY-NC-SA) are the only ones. LITFL is NC-SA. StatPearls and Eurasian J Emerg Med are ND.
- Guideline bodies allow reading but not embedding. ESC explicitly requires a licence for algorithmic or AI use; NICE requires an international fee licence; ACEP and AHA require written permission; ACR routes through NDSC.
- The practical model: build the ontology and rule logic in-house from primary literature (CDR derivation papers, JAMA RCE reviews, guideline recommendations expressed as facts, which are not copyrightable); license one structured content partner (BMJ Best Practice or UpToDate Connect) for interpretation text; use openly licensed text (WikEM, TJEM) with attribution.

## 7. Ranked: what to build on first

1. Rosen's 10e "Signs, Symptoms, and Presentations" section as the schema blueprint (critical / emergent / non-emergent per complaint).
2. Symptom to Diagnosis 5e as the template for pivotal findings, hypotheses, pretest probability and LR flow.
3. JAMA Rational Clinical Examination series plus McGee 5e for the LR layer; re-derive numbers from the cited primary studies.
4. Pines et al. Evidence-Based Emergency Care 3e (2023) as the index of validated ED decision rules; implement from the primary papers it cites.
5. ACEP Clinical Policies for which test or rule per complaint; low friction.
6. WikEM as the seed DDx list after written agreement on the AI clause.
7. Tintinalli 9e Türkçe and Semptomdan Tanıya Aciller for Turkish terminology and complaint taxonomy.
8. TATD guidelines and Turk J Emerg Med for Turkish-specific recommendations and local CDR validation.
9. ESC 2023 ACS, 2019 PE, 2018 syncope, 2024 aortic; AHA/ASA 2019 stroke; NICE NG232 and NG253, encoded as recommendation facts; budget for ESC and NICE licences if text or figures are displayed.
10. BMJ Best Practice or UpToDate Connect as the licensed deep-content partner.
